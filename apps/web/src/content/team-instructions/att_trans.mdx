## What it changes

What to do after winning the ball.

This decides what the team does in the moments after winning the ball. Counter-Attack pushes the block up and raises the chance of forward runs straight away, looking for a quick vertical pass into the disorganised opposition; Hold Shape keeps the formation and builds from the back. In the lab the block is pushed about 4 m per step in the few seconds after a win and supporting runs become more likely.

## Who needs which attributes

Counters need speed and timing: Pace and Acceleration to cover the ground, and Off the Ball so runs start at the right moment instead of an offside position. You also need someone who can play the first pass, usually a midfielder with Passing and Vision, and a forward who can hold or outrun the line. Without those outlets, Hold Shape is the more reliable setting.

## Pros / cons

- **Pros:** Counter: exploits a disorganised defence. Hold Shape: resets and takes control of possession.
- **Cons:** Counter without pace/outlets leads to quick turnovers.

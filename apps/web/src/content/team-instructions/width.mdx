## What it changes

How wide the team is in possession.

Attacking Width scales how far apart the team spreads across the pitch in possession. Wider settings pull wingers and full-backs towards the touchlines and drag the opposition's block sideways; narrower settings compress the team into the central lanes for short combinations. In the lab the lateral spread scales from about 0.68 to 1.24 of the standard shape, and it is the first thing to check when a team looks either stretched or crowded.

## Who needs which attributes

This instruction has no key attributes of its own. The roles you pick matter more: wide settings need wingers or wing-backs who beat their man or cross well (Dribbling, Crossing, Pace), and someone who can attack the box. Narrow settings need midfielders and inside forwards with good Passing, Technique and Off the Ball, plus full-backs able to provide the width the shape has given up.

## Pros / cons

- **Pros:** Wide: stretches the opponent’s block and opens inside channels. Narrow: central combinations.
- **Cons:** Wide makes reacting to a turnover harder (counter-press). Narrow makes it easier for the opponent to close the middle.

## What it changes

Immediate reaction to losing the ball.

Defensive Transition is the instant reaction after losing the ball. Counter-Press sends nearby players straight at the ball carrier for a few seconds to win it back before the opponent is organised; Regroup drops straight into the defensive shape. In the lab Counter-Press widens the press radius for about 4 s after a turnover, while Regroup adds drop-back movement.

## Who needs which attributes

Counter-Press needs players who can sprint and keep sprinting: Work Rate, Stamina and Acceleration, with Teamwork so the group reacts together. If players are spread across the pitch from a wide, aggressive attack, the first press can be played through and the opponent gets a counter. Regroup needs more discipline but fewer physical demands.

## Pros / cons

- **Pros:** Counter-Press: wins the ball back before the opponent organises. Regroup: protects the shape.
- **Cons:** Counter-Press with a very open/tired team gets played through in transition.

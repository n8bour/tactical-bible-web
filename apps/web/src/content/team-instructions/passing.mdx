## What it changes

Average pass length.

This sets how long the team's typical pass is. Shorter settings make players prefer the nearest safe teammate and keep the ball circulating; more direct settings make them look past the first line for a forward, a runner or a flick-on. In the lab the preferred pass length moves by roughly 7 m for each step away from Balanced, so the effect shows up as more passes into the next line at the short end and more long balls at the direct end.

## Who needs which attributes

The short end needs everyone to be comfortable on the ball: Passing, Technique, Composure and First Touch decide whether a pass under pressure reaches its target and is controlled cleanly. The direct end needs the receivers to exist: Heading and Strength for a target forward who wins the first ball, and Off the Ball and Pace for runners who attack the space the long pass is aimed at. If neither group is in your squad, stay near Balanced.

## Pros / cons

- **Pros:** Short: keeps the ball and tires the opponent. Direct: gets forward fast and ignores the press.
- **Cons:** Short demands Passing/Technique/Composure from everyone. Direct loses more balls without a target/pace up front.

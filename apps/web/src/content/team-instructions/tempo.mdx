## What it changes

Speed of decision-making on the ball.

Tempo controls how quickly players decide what to do on the ball. Higher settings shorten the time between receiving and releasing, so the team moves the ball before the opponent can reset; lower settings let players take an extra touch, scan and wait for the right moment. In the lab each step changes the decision interval by about 12 %, which is why high tempo looks hurried when the players are weak.

## Who needs which attributes

High tempo is a Decisions and Technique job: players have to choose well and execute quickly, and First Touch matters because there is less time to fix a bad reception. Stamina matters too, since a fast game costs more energy over 90 minutes. Low tempo asks for less from individuals but needs patience and a reason to wait, such as a settled shape and a front line that creates movement.

## Pros / cons

- **Pros:** High: catches the opponent disorganised. Low: control and patience.
- **Cons:** High + short passing demands high Decisions/Technique; more tiring.

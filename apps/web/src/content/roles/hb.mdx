## What it does by phase

Key moment (build): drops between the centre-backs (back 3).

When your side has the ball in its own half he steps back between the two centre-backs, turning a back four into a back three. That frees both full-backs to push on and gives the build-up an extra safe passer against a press. Once the ball moves up the pitch he does not run forward; he holds the middle of the pitch and covers the space behind the advancing full-backs, which is why the role is a rest-defence anchor as much as a passer.

## How attributes change it

Marking, Tackling and Positioning matter because he is defending in a back three the moment the ball is lost, often against a counter with the full-backs high. Anticipation and Concentration help him read the break and stay in the right gap. Composure is what lets him play out calmly when pressed by two strikers in the build-up. Strength and Jumping are useful because he will end up defending crosses and long balls against a bigger forward.

## Pairs well with

Lets both full-backs push on. Redundant with an IFB on the same side.

- Wing-Backs or Advanced Wing-Backs: with a third defender in place, both flanks can be left almost entirely to them.
- Ball-Playing Centre-Backs on either side, who can step wide and carry the ball forward.
- A Box-To-Box Midfielder or Central Midfielder ahead who becomes the main midfield link.
- Avoid an Inside Full-Back on the same side. Both players want the same central space and do the same job.

## Common mistakes

- Using him when the team has only two central defenders with no pace. When he drops, the back line must be able to handle a counter.
- Combining him with two Inside Full-Backs, which leaves no width at all and a crowded centre.
- Selecting a holding midfielder with weak Positioning and Decisions. He will drop at the wrong moments or not at all.

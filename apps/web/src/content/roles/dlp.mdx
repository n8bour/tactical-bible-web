## What it does by phase

Key moment (prog): stays back to distribute.

He sits in front of the defence and receives from the centre-backs, then moves the ball forward with the first or second pass instead of carrying it. The role is expressive, so he will look for the longer, riskier ball when a runner appears. He does not step into the final third; his job is to stay back and keep distributing from deep, which also leaves him as part of the rest-defence when possession is lost.

## How attributes change it

Passing, Vision and Decisions define this role. Vision sets how far and how wide he scans for a target and whether a through ball is on his radar at all, so a low-Vision DLP will keep playing the safe sideways pass. Decisions sets how quickly he picks and how often he picks the best of those options. First Touch and Technique control how cleanly he receives and weights the ball, and Composure protects his pass accuracy when the opposition presses him. Teamwork keeps him on his anchor position so the passing lanes stay where the team expects them.

## Pairs well with

Needs passing lanes: pair with an IWB or BPD. Avoid with "Bypass Press".

- Inside Wing-Back or Ball-Playing Centre-Back: they offer the angles and the secure first pass he needs.
- A Box-To-Box Midfielder or Central Midfielder alongside who does the running, so the DLP never has to leave his post.
- Forwards who move off the ball, such as a Channel Forward or Poacher, because long passes only matter if someone is running onto them.
- Avoid it with Bypass Press style plans: if the team is skipping the midfield, he has no ball to play with.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with no passing lanes: a flat back line and static forwards leave a playmaker with nobody to pass to.
- Playing him at high pressing opponents without Composure or First Touch. He will lose the ball in the most dangerous zone on the pitch.
- Putting a Deep-Lying Playmaker and a Midfield Playmaker in the same central pair when both want the ball and neither covers.
- Ignoring the defensive side. He is still a holding midfielder, so a low Positioning DLP leaves a hole in front of the back line.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays high, drifting into the channel.

Instead of waiting in the middle, the Splitting Outlet Attacking Midfielder stays high and drifts into the channel between the opposing centre-back and full-back. The opposition often leaves that space open when its full-backs push forward, so he is the target for a direct ball in behind. He contributes nothing defensively, so the rest of the team must be comfortable defending without him.

## How attributes change it

Pace and Acceleration decide whether he actually wins the race into the channel after the ball is won. Off the Ball and Anticipation set when he starts the run and whether he stays onside, and he needs both to exploit the gap before the full-back recovers. Dribbling and First Touch matter once he is through, so that he can carry the counter instead of losing the ball at speed. Stamina helps him repeat the sprint across the match.

## Pairs well with

Use it against teams whose full-backs attack aggressively, where the channel behind them is open. A deep, compact block with a Ball-Playing Centre-Back or Deep-Lying Playmaker who can hit the diagonal ball is a natural fit, and a Screening Defensive Midfielder helps cover the midfield space he does not defend. A Wide Outlet Winger on the opposite flank gives the counter a second runner.

## Common mistakes

- Playing him against a deep, narrow opponent with no channel to run into. He has no space and nothing happens.
- Giving the role to a slow player. The channel closes before he arrives.
- Having the team press high. He is already forward, so the counter is the plan, not the press.
- Stacking several outlets in one side, which crowds the same channel and leaves the midfield thin.

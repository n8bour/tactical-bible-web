## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays wide waiting for the counter.

When the team defends, the Wide Outlet Winger does not retreat. He stays high on the touchline near the opposition full-back, so that when the ball is won there is an immediate target in wide space. The other ten defend with one fewer player on that side, which is acceptable only if the team is happy to counter and the rest of the block is disciplined.

## How attributes change it

Pace and Acceleration are the point of the role: the long ball into space only works if he wins the race. Off the Ball decides when he starts the run once the ball is won and whether he stays onside, and Anticipation helps him react to the moment possession changes. First Touch and Dribbling matter once he receives, since a heavy touch at speed wastes the counter.

## Pairs well with

Use it with Counter-Attack and a deep or mid block, where winning the ball leads to a quick long pass. It is meant for one or two outlets at most; a Central Outlet Centre Forward or an Inside Outlet Winger on the other side can give the counter two targets without emptying the team. Behind him, a Holding Full-Back or Holding Wing-Back plus a Screening Defensive Midfielder cover the flank he leaves.

## Common mistakes

- Using more than two outlet roles, which leaves the midfield and defence outnumbered.
- Selecting a slow winger. If he cannot win the race, the outlet ball only turns the ball over.
- Playing him in a high-press or possession system. He is isolated up the pitch and contributes little to either phase.

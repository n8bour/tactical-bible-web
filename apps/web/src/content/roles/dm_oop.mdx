## What it does by phase

Standard defensive behaviour in front of the defence.

Out of possession he sits in the space just in front of the back line, covering the middle and stepping out to challenge when the ball is close. He neither drops into the defence nor pushes up to press, so he is the default holding option. He still moves with the ball, shifting across to keep the space between the lines closed.

## How attributes change it

Tackling, Positioning, Anticipation and Marking are the heart of this role. Positioning decides how close he stays to the ideal defensive spot, and Marking sets how tightly he picks up a nearby attacker. Anticipation shortens the delay before he reacts to a ball change, and Concentration keeps his read of the opposition accurate. Work Rate and Stamina are preferred because a holding midfielder covers a lot of ground in a defensive game.

## Pairs well with

The base option.

- A Central Midfielder alongside who handles more pressing, as the Defensive Midfielder stays behind.
- Standard Full-Backs or Centre-Backs: the base option fits neatly into a normal back line.
- A Dropping Defensive Midfielder is a variant when you need an extra centre-back, a Screening Defensive Midfielder when you need to close lanes more strictly.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the default as the right answer against every opponent. Against two strikers or a creative number ten, one of the more specific variants will do the job better.
- Pairing him with another holding player who has the same low press radius, which leaves no one to challenge the ball.
- Ignoring Positioning. A holding midfielder who is slightly out of position is worse than none.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): joins the striker in the box.

The Second Striker starts from the attacking-midfield line but behaves like a second forward: he stays high in build-up, makes runs beyond the defence while the team progresses, and arrives in the box to share the finishing. He is a goal threat first and a creator second, so he looks to shoot more readily than a typical Attacking Midfielder. His runs mean he is often the player attacking the space a dropping striker leaves behind.

## How attributes change it

Off the Ball and Anticipation set the quality of his runs: good ones start at the right moment and stay onside, poor ones start late or get caught beyond the last defender. Finishing and Composure decide whether the chance that his movement creates is converted, particularly under pressure in the box. Acceleration lets him win the first few metres when the ball is played behind, and First Touch keeps the move alive when the pass arrives awkwardly. In the lab, drop Off the Ball and Anticipation to see the offside risk and late starts appear.

## Pairs well with

He needs a partner who creates the space for him, so the natural match is a striker who drops: a Deep-Lying Forward, a Target Forward who holds the ball up, or a False Nine who pulls the centre-backs out. Wide support from a Winger (IP) or Wide Forward who stretches the defence also gives him room to arrive between the centre-backs.

## Common mistakes

- Playing him next to a Poacher or another forward who never drops. Both stay on the last line, nobody links play, and the midfield is left disconnected.
- Picking a Second Striker with weak Off the Ball and Anticipation. He gets flagged offside constantly or arrives after the chance has passed.
- Expecting him to be the main playmaker. His job is to score; if you need a creator, use an Advanced Playmaker or Free Role instead.

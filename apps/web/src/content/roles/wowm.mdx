## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays high and wide for the outlet.

When the opposition has the ball he stays high and wide, positioned as an outlet for the counter-attack. He does not track back into the midfield line, so the team defends with a player fewer in exchange for a ready target when the ball is won. The goal is a long ball to him when possession turns over.

## How attributes change it

Acceleration and Pace are the key attributes because he has to outrun the full-back once the ball is played over the top. Off the Ball gets him started at the right moment and avoids offside. First Touch and Dribbling are preferred since he has to control the long pass and carry it forward. Anticipation tells him when the ball is about to be won, and Stamina keeps him able to repeat the sprint.

## Pairs well with

Max. 2 outlets per tactic.

- A low or mid defensive block, where the team wins the ball deep and counters.
- A forward who also stays high, or a Central Outlet Centre Forward, so there are two outlets to hit.
- A Wide Midfielder or Tracking Wide Midfielder on the opposite flank, who stays in the midfield line so the team keeps its shape while one side is left high.

## Common mistakes

- Using more than two outlets in the same tactic. The team has no bodies in midfield and gets overrun.
- Playing him with a Full-Back who does not cover. The flank is left unguarded and the winger wins nothing.
- Choosing a slow player. If he cannot beat the full-back to the ball, the outlet is just a man standing out of the defence.

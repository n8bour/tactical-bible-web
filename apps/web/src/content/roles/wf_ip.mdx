## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): stays wide and attacks the far post.

The Wide Forward keeps his width on the flank like a winger, but plays like a forward: in progression he runs beyond the full-back in behind, and in the final third he drifts across to attack the far post as the ball comes from the other side. This gives the team a goal threat from a wide position and a natural target for a cross from the opposite flank.

## How attributes change it

Off the Ball sets the timing of his run into the box and whether he arrives at the far post unmarked. Finishing and Composure turn those arrivals into goals, while Pace and Acceleration let him run in behind the full-back when the ball is played through. Dribbling and First Touch help him beat his marker one-on-one when the move is not a through ball, and Crossing is a useful secondary attribute when he does deliver from the byline.

## Pairs well with

An Inside Wing-Back or Inside Full-Back behind him is the standard partner: it leaves the touchline to the Wide Forward and the full-back tucks into midfield, so the flank stays balanced. On the opposite wing, a Winger (IP) or Wing-Back who delivers crosses feeds his far-post runs, and a Target Forward or Centre Forward in the box occupies the centre-backs.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with a full-back who also stays wide. They duplicate the same lane and the central space stays empty.
- Expecting crossing volume. He attacks the box more than he supplies it, so a team without another crosser lacks service.
- Choosing a player with poor Off the Ball. His far-post run arrives too late or too early and gets caught offside.

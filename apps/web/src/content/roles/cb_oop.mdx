## What it does by phase

Defends on the line with balanced behaviour: steps out to the ball carrier when it makes sense.

He defends on the back line with balanced behaviour: he holds position and steps to a nearby ball carrier when the attacker is close enough to contest. He balances between stepping out and covering depth, so he rarely makes extreme decisions. The line looks after itself when both centre-backs share this role.

## How attributes change it

Marking, Tackling, Positioning, Heading and Strength cover the standard defensive actions, and Concentration keeps him alert. Anticipation and Decisions decide when to step out. Pace and Jumping reach matter for the situations that expose a centre-back. In the lab, low Positioning gives him an inaccurate defensive slot and low Anticipation delays his reaction to changes of play.

## Pairs well with

The base choice; adjust with his partner’s role (one steps out, the other covers).

Pair him with a partner whose role differs: Stopping Centre-Back steps while Covering Centre-Back covers, and the balanced one is the flexible half. A Defensive Midfielder in front keeps the back line from being isolated.

## Common mistakes

- Using two Stopping Centre-Backs to avoid a "balanced" choice; nobody covers.
- Expecting him to mark a specific striker by himself; the role is zonal and line-based.
- Ignoring Concentration and Anticipation in a team that defends many crosses and through balls.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): protects the central zone.

He holds the middle of the pitch, blocks the central passing lanes and presses less than a standard midfielder. He is the central midfield version of the screener: he protects the space in front of the back line and refuses to be pulled out wide. The result is a compact block that is hard to play through in the middle.

## How attributes change it

Positioning, Concentration and Decisions are the core of the role. Positioning decides how accurately he holds the screening slot, Concentration keeps his read of the opposition sharp for the whole match, and Decisions sets when he steps out to close a lane. Marking, Tackling, Anticipation and Teamwork are the preferred extras: they help him deal with a player who does arrive in his zone and keep him tied to the line.

## Pairs well with

Compact mid/low block.

- A Pressing Central Midfielder or Box-To-Box Midfielder beside him, so one presses and the other screens.
- A mid or low block with compact lines, which makes the lanes he closes harder to use.
- Wide midfielders or full-backs who cover the flanks, since he stays central.

## Common mistakes

- Using him in a high-press tactic. A midfielder who refuses to step out breaks the press.
- Playing two screening midfielders alongside each other, which leaves nobody to challenge the ball.
- Picking a player with weak Positioning. The role is only useful if he holds the right place.

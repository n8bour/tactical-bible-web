## What it does by phase

Key moment (prog): drops into midfield and drags centre-backs out.

The False Nine starts as the centre forward but drops deep into the attacking-midfield zone as the team progresses the ball. If the centre-backs follow him, a gap opens behind them for runners; if they do not, he has time and space to turn and play. In the final third he keeps dropping to create rather than staying in the box, so he is a creator first and a scorer only when the opportunity arises.

## How attributes change it

Vision sets how far and wide he scans for passes and whether he sees the through ball, and Passing and Decisions decide whether the ball is actually delivered to the runner. First Touch, Technique and Dribbling let him receive and turn between the lines, while Composure keeps him calm when the centre-backs step up. Teamwork and Off the Ball control how well his dropping fits the team shape instead of simply leaving the box.

## Pairs well with

Without runners the box stays empty, so he needs Inside Forwards, Inside Wingers, Channel Midfielders or a Second Striker who attack the space behind the defenders he drags out. A Deep-Lying Playmaker or Box-To-Box Midfielder behind him keeps the shape balanced, and a Wide Forward who attacks the far post gives the cross somewhere to go.

## Common mistakes

- Playing him with no one making forward runs, so he drags the centre-backs out and nobody takes advantage.
- Choosing a striker whose strengths are Finishing and Strength rather than Passing and Vision. He drops but cannot create.
- Using him in a team that wants to go direct with long balls to the front line. He is not there to win them.

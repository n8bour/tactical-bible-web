## What it does by phase

Balanced: supports the build-up and joins the attack in moderation.

He is the connector between defence and attack. In the build-up he drops to show for the ball and link with the centre-backs and defensive midfielder, then supports in the middle third and joins the attack in moderation without committing fully. He rarely does anything spectacular, but the other midfielders are free to specialise because he fills whichever gap the game opens.

## How attributes change it

Passing, Decisions and Teamwork are the key attributes: Passing keeps his short distribution accurate, Decisions sets how quickly and how reliably he picks the right option, and Teamwork holds him close to the tactical position instead of drifting. First Touch controls how cleanly he receives under pressure. Tackling matters because he is part of the pressing and defending midfield line. The preferred extras are Anticipation, Composure, Vision, Work Rate and Stamina, which make him more useful in both directions.

## Pairs well with

The glue that balances the midfield.

- A Defensive Midfielder or Half Back for a balanced double pivot: one covers while the other links.
- An Advanced Playmaker or Attacking Midfielder ahead of him, who benefit from a reliable simple passer.
- Wide players who pull the opposition out, so the central midfielder has room to receive and turn.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting creativity. The Central Midfielder is a balancing role, so if the team needs invention add a Midfield Playmaker or Advanced Playmaker.
- Playing two with identical duties and no holding player, which gives the midfield no clear structure.
- Choosing a player for his attacking numbers only. The role also needs Tackling, Work Rate and Teamwork to cover the middle.

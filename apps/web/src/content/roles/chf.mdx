## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): attacks the channel.

The Channel Forward runs into the gap between the opposing centre-back and full-back rather than staying in the middle. During progression he stretches the defence by running beyond it into the channel, and in the final third he stays there, pulling a centre-back sideways and leaving the central space open for a runner from midfield. He can also drift wide to receive the ball into feet.

## How attributes change it

Pace and Acceleration win the race when the ball is played into the channel, and Off the Ball controls the timing of the run and whether he stays onside against a defence holding a line. Dribbling and Finishing decide whether he can carry the move and finish from an angle. Work Rate keeps him active when the team has to restart the move, and it also helps him stay involved in the press.

## Pairs well with

He creates room for midfield runners, so pair him with a Second Striker, Attacking Midfielder (IP) or Advanced Playmaker who attacks the central space he vacates. An Inside Forward on the opposite flank adds another runner, and a Playmaking Winger or Midfield Playmaker can supply the diagonal passes.

## Common mistakes

- Using him alongside a winger who also stays on the touchline. They crowd the same channel and nobody attacks the middle.
- Picking a slow or low Off the Ball striker. Defenders recover and he is caught offside.
- Expecting him to hold the ball up. He needs space to run into, so a team with no passers to find him leaves him isolated.

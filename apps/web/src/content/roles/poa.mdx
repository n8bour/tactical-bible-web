## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): lives on the last line waiting for the ball.

The Poacher stays on the shoulder of the last defender throughout the move, running beyond the defence when the ball is played through and positioning himself in the middle of the box when it arrives. He does very little in build-up and rarely drops, preferring to shoot at the first chance. This gives him an exceptional goal threat but little involvement in link-up play.

## How attributes change it

Finishing and Composure decide whether the chance is converted, and Heading adds a second route to goal from crosses. Off the Ball and Anticipation control the timing of his runs and whether he stays onside while sitting on the defender, which is the main cost of playing on the last line. Concentration helps him keep his position and be in the right place when the chance finally comes after long periods without the ball.

## Pairs well with

He needs suppliers because he will not create for himself. Pair him with a Playmaking Winger, an Advanced Playmaker or a Winger (IP) who delivers crosses, and with an Attacking Midfielder (IP) or Second Striker who feeds him. A Wing-Back or Advanced Wing-Back adds the crossing volume he needs.

## Common mistakes

- Using him in a team without creative passers. He sits on the last line and never touches the ball.
- Combining him with another non-dropping striker, which leaves the midfield disconnected from the attack.
- Choosing a striker with weak Off the Ball and Anticipation. He sits too close to the defender and is flagged offside repeatedly.

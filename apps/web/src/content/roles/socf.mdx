## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays high, drifting into the channel.

The Splitting Outlet Centre Forward stays high and drifts into the channel between the opposing centre-back and full-back. He does not defend. When the ball is won, he is already in the space behind the advanced full-back, so a direct pass over the top starts the counter with the opposition's defence outnumbered or out of position.

## How attributes change it

Pace and Acceleration decide whether he wins the race into the channel, and Off the Ball and Anticipation control when he starts the run and whether he stays onside. Dribbling and First Touch matter once he receives because he often runs onto the ball at speed. Stamina lets him repeat the run over the match, since he spends most of the defensive phase waiting and sprinting.

## Pairs well with

Use it against teams whose full-backs push very high. Pair him with a Ball-Playing Centre-Back or Deep-Lying Playmaker who can hit the diagonal ball, and with a Screening Defensive Midfielder to protect the block. A Wide Outlet Winger on the other side gives the counter a second target without needing a third outlet.

## Common mistakes

- Using him against a team with conservative full-backs. There is no channel to run into and he is wasted.
- Choosing a slow striker, so the defence recovers before he gets there.
- Combining him with a Central Outlet Centre Forward, which stacks two strikers who both stay high and removes any pressure on the opposition's build-up.

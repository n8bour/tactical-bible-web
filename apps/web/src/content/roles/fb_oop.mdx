## What it does by phase

Standard flank defending, holds the line.

He defends the flank in a standard way: he holds the line with the centre-backs, tracks the nearest threat on his side and steps out to the ball carrier when the ball is close. He neither presses high like a Pressing Full-Back nor retreats as far as a Holding Full-Back. It is the neutral option for a team that wants its full-backs to simply defend the flank.

## How attributes change it

Marking, Tackling, Positioning, Concentration and Anticipation make up the core, and they decide how reliably he closes the winger and keeps his line. Decisions and Teamwork refine when he steps out and how closely he holds his position, while Acceleration helps him recover. In the lab, low Positioning shows as an inaccurate slot in the defensive line and low Anticipation delays his reaction.

## Pairs well with

A balanced base.

Any in-possession full-back role works with him, because he does not change the team's shape. Pair him with a Centre-Back who covers the space behind, such as a Covering Centre-Back, and a Wide Covering Central Midfielder if the winger is dangerous. A Pressing Full-Back on the opposite side gives you an asymmetric option.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting him to press the winger high up the pitch; for that you need Pressing Full-Back.
- Leaving him alone against a fast winger with no cover; Pace and Acceleration still matter.
- Picking him for a back 5, where Wing-Back is the proper full-width role.

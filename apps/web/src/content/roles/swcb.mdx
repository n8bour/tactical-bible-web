## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): steps out aggressively into the half-space.

In a back 3 the wide centre-back steps out aggressively into the half-space and channel to press the forward or attacking midfielder who receives there. Unlike in a back 4 he has colleagues covering centrally, so he has more freedom to go. It turns the back line into a pressing line.

## How attributes change it

Tackling, Aggression, Anticipation, Acceleration and Work Rate are the key set: he must read the receiver, close the space fast and keep pressing. Strength and Bravery help when he arrives in a physical contest, and Marking helps when he has to stay with his man. Work Rate scales how far he follows in the lab.

## Pairs well with

Very good against opposing AMCL/AMCR. More freedom than in a back 4.

Covering Centre-Back in the centre to protect the space behind. Opposing AMCL/AMCR are the ideal target. A Pressing Wing-Back or Holding Wing-Back outside him balances the flank.

## Common mistakes

- Using him against a team that plays direct: when he steps, the ball goes behind.
- Playing a low-pace defender who cannot recover.
- Pressing with him but leaving the wing-back unsupported; the flank becomes open.

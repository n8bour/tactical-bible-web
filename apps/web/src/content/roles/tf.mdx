## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): aerial target in the box.

The Target Forward stays on the last line as a physical focal point. Long balls from the build-up are aimed at him to win the duel and hold the ball while the team moves up, and crosses are aimed at him inside the box. He pulls players onto his back, lays the ball off to supporting runners and finishes from headers and close range.

## How attributes change it

Jumping Reach and Heading decide whether he wins the aerial duel, while Strength and Bravery let him hold off the defender and keep going in a challenge. Balance helps him stay on his feet when the contact comes, and Finishing and Composure convert what he wins. First Touch and Teamwork matter when the ball is laid off rather than headed on, since he needs to control it and bring other players into the move.

## Pairs well with

Pair him with crossing roles such as a Winger (IP), Advanced Wing-Back or Wing-Back, and with the team instruction Bypass Press so the ball reaches him quickly. A Second Striker or Inside Forward who attacks the second ball benefits from his flick-ons and lay-offs, and a Channel Midfielder can arrive from deep.

## Common mistakes

- Using him in a short-passing team that rarely crosses or goes long. He wins nothing because the ball never goes in the air.
- Picking a tall striker with poor Strength and Bravery, who still loses the aerial duel under contact.
- Playing him with nobody close by. A win in the air is wasted if no runner picks up the second ball.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): cuts inside towards goal.

The Inside Forward starts out wide but his movement is always towards the centre. In build-up and progression he stretches the opposing full-back, then in the final third he cuts inside onto his stronger foot to shoot or play a through ball, and he makes runs beyond the defence when the ball is in the middle. He is an attacker first, so he shoots and dribbles more than a traditional winger and delivers few crosses.

## How attributes change it

Dribbling, Agility and Acceleration decide whether the cut inside actually beats the defender, while First Touch and Technique keep the ball under control when he receives on the move. Finishing and Composure turn the space he creates into goals, and Composure especially helps when the shot is taken with a defender closing. Off the Ball controls the timing of his diagonal runs into the box, and Flair pushes him towards the more ambitious option, which can unlock a defence but costs possession if Decisions is weak.

## Pairs well with

Give him a Wing-Back (IP), Advanced Wing-Back or Full-Back (IP) on the same side, who provides the width he abandons by cutting inside. A Deep-Lying Forward, Target Forward or False Nine in the middle helps drag defenders away and links play, and a Channel Midfielder can cover the half-space behind him.

## Common mistakes

- Pairing him with an Inside Full-Back or Inside Wing-Back on the same flank. Both leave the touchline and the opposition full-back is never stretched.
- Choosing weak Finishing and Composure. He runs into the same crowded central space as everyone else and produces poor shots.
- Playing him on the wrong side for his preferred foot, which turns the cut inside into a harder, weaker shot.
- Expecting crosses. If you need a supplier from wide, use a Winger (IP) instead.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays behind the line covering depth.

He sits slightly behind the line so that anything played over the top lands in his zone. He does not step out to challenge, which means his partner takes those duels and he supports with cover. When the line is high this protects against the long ball.

## How attributes change it

Positioning, Anticipation and Concentration decide whether he reads a ball over the top early; Decisions help him choose to drop. Pace and Acceleration are essential: he is the one chasing the runner, so speed is what the role asks for. Marking, Tackling and Composure are secondary, only needed when the cover duty turns into a duel. Low Positioning gives him a visibly wrong slot.

## Pairs well with

The classic partner of a Stopping Centre-Back. Ideal with a high line and a quick centre-back.

Stopping Centre-Back is the classic partner: one steps, one covers. A Sweeper Keeper and a high Defensive Line suit a quick Covering Centre-Back. A Pressing Defensive Midfielder or Pressing Central Midfielder in front can be pushed forward knowing he is behind.

## Common mistakes

- Using two Covering Centre-Backs, who both sit deep and invite pressure on the edge of the box.
- Choosing a slow centre-back for a high line; the role depends on Pace.
- Expecting him to win aerial duels in the box; he is cover, not a stopper.

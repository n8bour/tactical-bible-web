## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): drops into the defensive line.

When the opposition has the ball he drops into the line of centre-backs, forming a back five with the full-backs. This gives you an extra body in the penalty area and in front of the goal. He sits narrow and central, which is why the role works best against sides with two strikers. The deeper position limits his ability to step out into midfield, so he is a defensive, low-block role.

## How attributes change it

Marking, Tackling and Positioning matter because he is now a centre-back for defensive phases, picking up forwards and holding the gap between the centre-backs. Heading, Jumping and Strength are key for dealing with crosses and long balls in the box. Concentration helps him stay locked in during long spells without touching the ball. Anticipation and Decisions are the preferred attributes because they let him choose when to step out and when to hold.

## Pairs well with

Low block against 2 strikers.

- A back four or back three with cautious Full-Backs: the extra man makes a back five without needing a Wing-Back.
- Counter-attacking Wide Outlet Wide Midfielders or an outlet forward, who stay high while the rest of the team defends.
- A low or mid defensive line and a deeper line of engagement, so the whole team shares the same low block.

## Common mistakes

- Playing him in a high-pressing system. When he drops that deep, the gap between the lines becomes huge.
- Using him with weak Heading or Strength in a defence that will be attacked from the wings. He becomes the weak link in the area.
- Combining him with a Screening or Dropping partner, which leaves no one in the middle to engage with.

## What it does by phase

Holds position but is expressive on the ball: looks for progressive passes and switches of play.

He still holds his defensive position but becomes the team's first progressive passer. He looks for passes into midfield between the lines, diagonal balls to a winger and switches to the far side when play is crowded. If given space he steps forward with the ball briefly, but he does not leave the back line.

## How attributes change it

Passing, Composure and Decisions are the heart of the role: they decide the quality and the choice of each pass. Vision (a preferred attribute) extends how far he looks and whether he sees through balls and switches. He is still a centre-back, so Heading, Marking, Tackling, Positioning and Strength must be good enough that the defending does not suffer. In the lab, reducing his Passing visibly widens the angle error on his long passes and his Composure shows when an opponent closes him down.

## Pairs well with

A BPD + a simple CB is the most stable pairing. Two BPDs only with a safe defensive midfielder.

A No-Nonsense Centre-Back or plain Centre-Back as partner is the most stable. Two Ball-Playing Centre-Backs work only with a safe Defensive Midfielder who stays in front of them. Goalkeeper options such as Ball-Playing Goalkeeper help create a clean first line.

## Common mistakes

- Using him against a high press with poor Composure, which produces turnovers in front of goal.
- Playing two of them with two attacking full-backs; the rest-defence loses its safety.
- Choosing him for Passing but ignoring Marking and Positioning; he gets caught on the counter.

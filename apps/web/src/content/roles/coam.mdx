## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays high and central for the outlet.

When the team is defending, the Central Outlet Attacking Midfielder does not drop back with the block. He holds a high central position near the halfway line or beyond, so the first pass after winning the ball has an obvious target. The cost is that the team effectively defends with one fewer player in midfield, which is why the role belongs in a compact low or mid block that invites the opposition forward.

## How attributes change it

Off the Ball and Anticipation drive how well he positions himself for the outlet pass and how early he starts his run once the ball is won, and they also affect offside risk if he goes too soon. Acceleration lets him turn a won ball into an immediate counter, while First Touch decides whether the long ball is controlled or gets away from him. Composure and Decisions help once he is through, when the next action has to be chosen quickly.

## Pairs well with

It suits a low block with Counter-Attack, where the team is happy to defend deep and break fast. Put a Ball-Playing Centre-Back or Deep-Lying Playmaker behind him to find him quickly, and keep a Screening Defensive Midfielder or Tracking Attacking Midfielder nearby to protect the space he leaves. A Central Outlet Centre Forward would stack two central outlets, so use it only if you want a very top-heavy counter.

## Common mistakes

- Using the role in a possession or high-press system. The player is stranded high up when the ball is lost and does nothing in the press.
- Using too many outlets across the team. Only one or two players should stay high, otherwise the midfield is short-handed.
- Choosing a slow, weak-First-Touch player. Outlet passes turn into lost possession instead of counters.
- Forgetting there is no defending duty, so a mid-block with a narrow midfield becomes easy to play through.

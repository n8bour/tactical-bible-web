## What it does by phase

Standard defending for a winger.

The Winger holds his wide position in the team shape and presses the opposing full-back when the ball arrives in his zone. He does not track back as deep as a Tracking Winger, nor does he stay high like an outlet, so he balances the attacking duty of the role with a reasonable contribution to the block. Behind him the full-back covers the flank.

## How attributes change it

Work Rate decides how far he closes down and how readily he recovers into the block, and Stamina lets him keep doing so late in the game. Teamwork keeps him linked to the rest of the line when the shape shifts across the pitch. Tackling and Positioning help when he does have to defend a duel, and Anticipation lets him react earlier when the ball switches to his flank.

## Pairs well with

It is the base option and fits almost any system. Pair it with a Full-Back (OOP) or Pressing Full-Back behind him so the flank is shared sensibly, and with a Screening Central Midfielder or Defensive Midfielder who covers the space inside.

## Common mistakes

- Selecting it for a low-Work-Rate player and then asking the team to press. The winger barely moves and the flank is exposed.
- Leaving it in a deep block where you actually need a Tracking Winger to follow the opposing full-back.
- Using it with a full-back who also does not defend, which leaves two players who both hold back from the same threat.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): attacks the channel between centre-back and full-back.

He starts as a central midfielder but breaks forward into the channel between the opposing centre-back and full-back when the team gets into the final third. It is a discreet game-breaker: he does not show up on the stat sheet in build-up, but his late runs into the gap stretch the defence and create space for others. The run is made with the ball played into the channel for him, or to drag defenders away from a centre forward.

## How attributes change it

Off the Ball is the most important attribute because it decides when he starts the run, with Anticipation helping him avoid being offside. Acceleration lets him cover the gap before the defender closes it, and Dribbling and First Touch make sure he does not lose the ball when it arrives. Work Rate and Stamina are listed because he makes the run, then has to track back. Passing and Technique cover his role as a midfielder in the build-up.

## Pairs well with

Pairs with a false 9 who frees up space.

- A False Nine, who drops deep and drags a centre-back out of the line so the channel opens.
- A Wing-Back or Full-Back who stays wide, so the central midfielder can attack the half-space rather than the touchline.
- A Defensive Midfielder behind him to cover while he is in the final third.

## Common mistakes

- Playing him with low Off the Ball or Acceleration. The run will be too slow or badly timed and wastes the role.
- Using him with a Target Forward who stays in the channel himself. Both players want the same space.
- Forgetting the defensive recovery. A midfielder who sprints into the channel needs the stamina to return.

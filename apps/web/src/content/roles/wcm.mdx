## What it does by phase

Key moment (prog): moves out to the flank from midfield.

In possession he drifts from the middle out to the flank, effectively becoming a wide midfielder in progression. This gives the team an extra width option from midfield, and often replaces the old Mezzala or Carrilero movement. The key moment comes in the progression phase, when he offers an outlet on the side and makes room for others to play inside. When the ball is lost he has to come back to the centre.

## How attributes change it

Passing, First Touch and Technique keep the ball moving when he receives out wide. Off the Ball and Teamwork determine how well he times the move out and fits into the team's shape. Work Rate and Stamina are essential because he covers the full width of the pitch and has to recover. Crossing, Dribbling and Vision are the preferred extras: they make his arrival on the flank dangerous rather than just a place to pass the ball.

## Pairs well with

Perfect with a winger who comes inside or an IWB.

- An Inside Winger or Inside Forward on the same side: the winger moves in and the midfielder takes over the width.
- An Inside Wing-Back or Full-Back who stays narrower, so the flank is not overloaded.
- A Defensive Midfielder in the centre who covers the space left behind.

## Common mistakes

- Pairing him with a Winger who stays wide. Two players on the same touchline crowd each other and the centre is empty.
- Using him with low Work Rate or Stamina, so he can neither support out wide nor recover to the middle.
- Expecting him to behave as a central playmaker. He gives up the centre to create width, so the team needs someone else to organise there.

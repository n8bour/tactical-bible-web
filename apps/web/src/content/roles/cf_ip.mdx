## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): pins the centre-backs and finishes.

The Centre Forward is the standard complete striker. He stays central, pins the opposing centre-backs, and links the play by holding up the ball or laying it off before arriving in the box to finish. He neither drops as deep as a Deep-Lying Forward nor sits on the last line like a Poacher, so he blends into most attacking structures without demanding special support.

## How attributes change it

Finishing and Composure convert the chances he gets, while First Touch and Technique let him bring the ball under control with a defender on his back and lay it off. Strength protects the ball in those duels, and Off the Ball plus Acceleration decide whether he can get away from his marker when the final pass is played. Passing and Decisions add to the link-up play when the team relies on him to combine rather than just score.

## Pairs well with

Because the role is balanced, it pairs well with a Second Striker or Attacking Midfielder (IP) who runs beyond him, and with Inside Forwards or Wingers (IP) who supply the ball from the flanks. A Deep-Lying Playmaker or Midfield Playmaker behind keeps the supply regular.

## Common mistakes

- Pairing him with another striker who does exactly the same job. Both stay central and the team lacks either a runner or a link.
- Picking a player with weak First Touch and Strength. He cannot hold the ball, so the build-up breaks down when it reaches him.
- Expecting him to create like a False Nine. The role links, but he is primarily a goal threat.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): goes all the way to the byline, almost like a winger.

He goes all the way to the byline, almost like a winger, and often arrives in the box. In the final third he can be the widest attacker. Behind him the flank is wide open.

## How attributes change it

Crossing, Dribbling, Technique and Off the Ball make him an attacker in all but name: he must beat a man, deliver from the byline and time his arrival. Acceleration and Pace get him to the byline and back, while Stamina and Work Rate decide whether he can repeat the run all match. In the lab, higher Off the Ball and Anticipation improve the timing of his run and lower the risk of straying offside. Marking and Tackling are not listed as key here, so he is a poor defender of the space he leaves.

## Pairs well with

Leaves the flank exposed: compensate with a Covering Wide CB / Wide Covering CM.

A Covering Wide Centre-Back or Covering Centre-Back and a Wide Covering Central Midfielder, to protect the flank he abandons. An Inside Forward or Inside Winger ahead of him vacates the touchline and gives him the byline for himself. A Holding Wing-Back or Holding Full-Back on the opposite side balances the risk.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with no cover behind, which hands the opposition an open flank on every turnover.
- Playing him with a Winger who stays on the touchline, so two players want the same lane.
- Ignoring Stamina and Work Rate; a tired Advanced Wing-Back stops getting forward and stops getting back.

## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays on the goal line.

He stays on or very near the goal line and reacts to shots rather than anticipating danger. That keeps him positioned for long-range efforts and means he rarely makes a rash dash from his line. It also leaves the area behind the defence to whoever cleans it up.

## How attributes change it

Positioning and Concentration keep him in the right place on the line, while Reflexes and Handling decide the saves. Agility helps him cover the goal quickly. One-on-Ones and Decisions are only preferred because the role asks him to avoid the decision to come out; they matter when he is forced to. A keeper with low Positioning will drift off the correct angle even though he is staying home.

## Pairs well with

Works best with a low defensive line; with a high line it leaves huge space in behind.

A low Defensive Line with a Holding Full-Back or Holding Wing-Back, a Covering Centre-Back and a Screening Defensive Midfielder: everything is built to keep play in front of the keeper. A back 5 sitting in a low block plays naturally into his strengths.

## Common mistakes

- Using him behind a high Defensive Line. The space in behind becomes enormous, because he will not come out to cover it.
- Choosing him for a keeper with weak Handling, since parried shots from a deep line are frequently punished.
- Forgetting that a line-holder gives away through balls; pair it with defenders who keep the line compact.

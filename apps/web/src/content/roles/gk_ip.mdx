## What it does by phase

A balanced goalkeeper in possession: distributes safely according to the team instructions.

Think of him as the deepest rest-defence player: he stays central on his line, shows for short backpasses and restarts play according to your Goal Kicks and short-distribution settings rather than his own initiative. In the build phase he is an outlet when the centre-backs are pressed, but he rarely progresses the ball himself. Once the ball is beyond halfway he simply holds a safe position and sweeps up loose clearances.

## How attributes change it

Handling, Reflexes, Positioning and Concentration decide whether he is a good shot-stopper, and those matter most here because this role asks little of his feet. Kicking and Throwing set how accurately he restarts play, and Composure and Decisions decide whether a short pass under pressure is a good one or a gift. In the lab, a keeper with low Decisions picks his distribution more randomly and slowly, so a team that wants to play out from the back will visibly stall.

## Pairs well with

A solid base when the keeper is not good with his feet; pairs with Goal Kicks "Mixed".

Pair him with No-Nonsense Centre-Back or Centre-Back in IP and a Defensive Midfielder who shows for the short ball. Goal Kicks "Mixed" lets him go short when it is safe and long when it is not, which matches his limited passing. He also suits a Target Forward or Deep-Lying Forward who can take the long option.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with "Play Through Press" when his Passing, First Touch and Composure are poor; he will be forced to play risky balls he cannot execute.
- Expecting him to start attacks. He is a safe base, not a creator, so use Ball-Playing Goalkeeper if you need that.
- Ignoring Kicking and Throwing: a keeper with weak distribution wastes the possession his defenders won.

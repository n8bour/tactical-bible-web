## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): drops back to defend.

The Tracking Attacking Midfielder gives up his advanced position as soon as the ball is lost and falls back into the midfield block, following runners and covering space in front of the defence. He becomes a second midfield line rather than a free attacker, which makes the team much harder to play through centrally. The price is that his energy and position are far from the attack when possession is regained.

## How attributes change it

Work Rate sets how far he tracks runners and how quickly he gets back to the block, and Stamina decides whether he can keep doing it for ninety minutes. Teamwork keeps him on his tactical position inside the shape rather than chasing the ball, and Positioning controls how accurately he occupies his defensive slot. Tackling and Marking matter once opponents arrive in his zone, since he is expected to win duels as well as screen.

## Pairs well with

It is designed for a low block 4-5-1, where he sits with the central midfielders and shields the back line. Combine him with a Screening Defensive Midfielder or Wide Covering Central Midfielder behind him, and with Tracking Wingers on the flanks so the whole line drops together. A Central Outlet Centre Forward up front can keep the counter-attack option alive.

## Common mistakes

- Using it for a creative, low-stamina playmaker. He arrives tired in the block and has nothing left when the ball is won.
- Running it in a high-press shape. He is dropping deep while the rest of the team pushes up, which stretches the team apart.
- Choosing a Tracking Attacking Midfielder with poor Positioning or Tackling, so he follows runners but loses the duel.

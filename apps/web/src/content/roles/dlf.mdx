## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): drops to link play.

The Deep-Lying Forward drops off the last line into the space between the opposing midfield and defence, receives the ball with his back to goal and lays it off to runners. In progression he comes deeper to be an outlet for the build-up, and in the final third he keeps dropping to link, rather than staying with the centre-backs. He retains a goal threat but his main job is to connect midfield and attack.

## How attributes change it

First Touch and Technique let him control the ball under pressure between the lines, and Strength with Balance keeps him from being pushed off it. Passing, Vision and Decisions determine whether the lay-off is the right one and whether he spots the run, while Composure lets him make that choice with a defender closing. Off the Ball and Anticipation help him time the moment he drops and the moment he turns to attack the box.

## Pairs well with

He needs teammates who attack the space he leaves. Pair him with an Inside Forward, a Channel Midfielder or a Second Striker who runs beyond him, and a Winger (IP) who supplies the width. A Box-To-Box Midfielder arriving from deep also benefits from the extra connection.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with a team that has nobody running in behind. He drops, the centre-backs follow, and the space is never attacked.
- Picking a striker with poor First Touch or Strength. The ball is lost on his back and the counter starts.
- Pairing him with another dropping striker such as a False Nine, which leaves no one in the box.

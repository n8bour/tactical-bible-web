## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): positions himself high to sweep balls in behind.

He plays far higher than a standard keeper, acting as an extra centre-back who intercepts through balls and long passes before attackers get a run on goal. With a high Defensive Line he is the reason a ball in behind is not an automatic chance. When the ball is in the opponent's half he is positioned near the edge of his box, which gives the team an extra passing option too.

## How attributes change it

Command of Area, One-on-Ones and Rushing Out decide how far from his line he is willing and able to begin. Anticipation, Decisions and Acceleration decide whether he gets to the ball first; a slow or hesitant sweeper is the most exposed player on the pitch. In the lab, Rushing Out scales how high he starts from his line, so lowering it pulls him back. Composure and Passing matter because the ball often ends at his feet rather than being cleared.

## Pairs well with

A must with a high defensive line. Needs Anticipation, Decisions and Rushing Out.

A high Defensive Line and a high Line of Engagement with a pressing front line; the sweeper makes the compression safe. Pair him with a Covering Centre-Back and a pacey Stopping Centre-Back who can both step and recover. Ball-Playing Goalkeeper is the natural in-possession role to continue the same philosophy.

## Common mistakes

- Using him behind a deep line; he has no space to sweep and gains only risk.
- Picking a keeper with low Anticipation or Decisions; he will rush out late or at the wrong moment.
- Expecting him to protect against long shots; he is positioned too high to cover a lofted effort.

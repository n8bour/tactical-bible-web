## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): stays high and inside.

The Inside Outlet Winger does not defend the touchline. He stays high and tucked into the half-space, ready to attack the central channel the moment the ball is won. Compared with a Wide Outlet Winger, he is closer to the goal and to the other forwards, so the counter-attack tends to arrive in the middle where finishing chances are better.

## How attributes change it

Off the Ball and Anticipation drive how early he reads the turnover and starts his run, along with offside risk. Pace and Acceleration win the race into the central space before the recovering defenders arrive. Finishing and Composure matter because he often ends the counter himself, and First Touch and Dribbling help when the ball arrives at speed or under pressure.

## Pairs well with

It works with Counter-Attack and a mid or low block, where winning the ball leads to a quick forward pass. Combine him with a Wide Outlet Winger on the opposite side or a Central Outlet Centre Forward, but keep the total number of outlets at two. A Screening Defensive Midfielder or Tracking Attacking Midfielder protects the space he leaves unguarded.

## Common mistakes

- Using him in a possession system, where he sits high and inside while the team is trying to build slowly, which clogs the midfield.
- Choosing a player with poor Finishing, so the counters reach the box but end in weak shots.
- Combining him with too many other outlets and leaving the defence outnumbered when the counter breaks down.

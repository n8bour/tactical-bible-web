## What it does by phase

Standard defensive behaviour: alternates between staying on the line and coming out depending on the situation.

He stays on his line for shots and close-range threats and comes out for through balls and crosses when the situation looks winnable. His starting depth and willingness to rush out follow the keeper's attributes, not a fixed instruction, so the role adapts to how high your team defends. It is the neutral option between Line-Holding Keeper and Sweeper Keeper.

## How attributes change it

Handling, Reflexes, Positioning and Concentration are the shot-stopping core. Agility and One-on-Ones matter in close duels. Rushing Out scales how far he is willing to start from his line, and Command of Area and Aerial Reach decide how many crosses he can claim. In the lab, a higher Reflexes value shortens his reaction to a shot, and a higher Aerial Reach lets him claim crosses from further away.

## Pairs well with

A safe choice with a medium defensive line.

A medium Defensive Line with a Covering Centre-Back and Stopping Centre-Back pair, who handle balls in front of him. A Defensive Midfielder or Screening Defensive Midfielder in front of the back line shields him from shots from the edge of the box.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with a very high line and expecting him to sweep; he will not start high enough, so choose Sweeper Keeper.
- Using him in a deep low block expecting him to hold the goal line; Line-Holding Keeper does that more reliably.
- Treating attributes like Rushing Out as irrelevant; a keeper with poor Rushing Out will hesitate on through balls.

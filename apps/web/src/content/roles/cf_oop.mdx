## What it does by phase

Standard defensive behaviour for a striker.

The Centre Forward in the defensive phase is the first line of the team's defence. He presses the opposing centre-backs and goalkeeper when the trigger is on, and otherwise holds a central position that blocks the pass into the opposing midfield. He does not drop deep like a Tracking Centre Forward, nor stays high and avoids defending like an outlet.

## How attributes change it

Work Rate sets how far he closes down and how long he keeps pressing, and Teamwork keeps him on the team's pressing shape instead of chasing the ball alone. Anticipation determines how early he reads a pass to the centre-back and gets there before it. Stamina and Aggression help keep the press going throughout the match, and Acceleration lets him close down the goalkeeper or defender before they play it.

## Pairs well with

It is the base option and fits most systems. Combine it with an Attacking Midfielder (OOP) and Pressing Central Midfielder so the press moves as a unit, or with Winger (OOP) players who close the full-backs. In a more defensive system, a second striker playing as a Tracking Centre Forward adds more defensive help.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a striker with poor Work Rate in a high-press system. He is not closing anyone down and the opposition plays around him.
- Mixing it with a high-trigger press and unfit players. The front line tires and the gap behind it grows.
- Using it when you actually need a counter-attacking target. The Central Outlet Centre Forward is the role for that.

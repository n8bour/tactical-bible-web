## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): tracks back.

The Tracking Winger gives up his advanced position to follow the opposing full-back all the way back into his own half. In a 4-5-1 this means the wide midfielder defends like a full-back, doubling up on the opposition winger and preventing overlaps. Because the whole flank is covered, the defending full-back can stay narrower, but the winger starts any counter from a very deep position.

## How attributes change it

Work Rate scales how far he follows the full-back and how quickly he gets back, and Stamina keeps him able to do this repeatedly without fading. Teamwork and Positioning keep him in the right slot in the block when he is not directly tracking someone, and Marking sets how tightly he stays with his man. Tackling matters when the duel happens, and Anticipation helps him get to the ball before the overlap is completed.

## Pairs well with

Use it in a mid or low block with a 4-5-1. A Holding Full-Back or standard Full-Back (OOP) behind him lets the pair cover the flank between them, and a Screening Defensive Midfielder or Tracking Attacking Midfielder in the middle gives the block its compactness. A Central Outlet Centre Forward or Wide Outlet Winger on the other side keeps a counter option available.

## Common mistakes

- Using a Tracking Winger in a high press. He drops deep while the rest of the team pushes up, so the lines stretch.
- Choosing a player with poor Stamina or Work Rate. He tires, stops tracking and the opposition's overlap gets in behind.
- Playing him when the opposition full-back is conservative. There is no runner to follow, so he only wastes energy and his attacking contribution.

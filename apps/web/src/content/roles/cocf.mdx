## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): acts as an outlet.

The Central Outlet Centre Forward stays high and central while the team defends. He does not press or drop, so the rest of the team defends with ten players while he waits on the shoulder of the opposing centre-backs. When the ball is won, a long pass up to him starts the counter and gives the team time to move up.

## How attributes change it

Off the Ball and Anticipation determine how well he positions himself for the outlet pass and when he starts his run, while keeping him onside. Strength lets him shield the ball when it arrives and hold it up while the team joins him. Composure helps him decide quickly what to do once he has the ball, and First Touch and Acceleration let him control it and get away before the defenders recover.

## Pairs well with

It is best in a low block with Counter-Attack. A Ball-Playing Centre-Back, Deep-Lying Playmaker or Midfield Playmaker who can play long accurate passes feeds him, and Wide Outlet Winger or Inside Outlet Winger runners can support him from the flanks. Keep to one or two outlets in total and let a Screening Defensive Midfielder protect the defence.

## Common mistakes

- Using the role in a possession or high-press team. He is far from the play and takes no part in the press.
- Selecting a striker with poor Strength and First Touch. The ball is lost when it reaches him.
- Using more than two outlet roles, which leaves the rest of the team stretched and outnumbered.

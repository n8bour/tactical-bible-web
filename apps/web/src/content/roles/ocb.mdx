## What it does by phase

Key moment (final): overlaps down the flank like a wing-back.

In a back 3 the wide centre-back takes a wing-back's role in the final third: when the team is in possession he moves up the flank, overlapping the winger or wing-back and delivering crosses. This stretches the opposition and gives an additional wide attacker with a defender's positional discipline. It leaves the back line short, so it needs the others to hold.

## How attributes change it

Crossing and Dribbling make his overlap useful, because he is delivering or beating a man in an advanced area. Stamina, Work Rate and Pace carry him up and back, and Acceleration lets him close a gap if he is caught. Marking, Tackling and Positioning are still the defender's core since he is first and foremost a centre-back. Off the Ball is preferred as it tells him when to break into the channel.

## Pairs well with

Only makes sense in a back 3. Needs stamina and a wing-back who comes inside so they do not get in each other’s way.

Only in a back 3 or back 5. An Inside Wing-Back or Inside Full-Back on his side who comes inside lets him have the flank to himself. A Covering Wide Centre-Back on the other side and a Defensive Midfielder who covers the vacated channel keep the structure sound.

## Common mistakes

- Using him in a flat back 4; there is no cover when he leaves.
- Combining him with a Wing-Back who also stays wide on the same side, so both take the flank.
- Selecting a defender with poor Stamina, who tires and leaves an exposed side.

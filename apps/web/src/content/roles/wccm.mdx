## What it does by phase

Key moment (oop): covers the full-back’s flank.

When the full-back or wing-back on one side has pushed forward, he covers that flank. This is the old Carrilero job: he shifts from the middle across to the wide channel so that a counter down that side meets a player. He makes the full-back's aggressive overlaps safe to attempt, which means the team can attack with the width of a wing-back and still be covered.

## How attributes change it

Work Rate and Stamina come first because he moves laterally across the pitch many times in a match. Teamwork keeps the middle compact while he covers wide, and Positioning keeps him in the right place to cover the gap. Tackling lets him win the ball in the wide channel. Acceleration, Anticipation and Pace are the preferred attributes: they let him reach a winger who is about to run in behind.

## Pairs well with

The Advanced Wing-Back’s partner.

- An Advanced Wing-Back, whose overlaps leave the flank empty, so the covering midfielder is the safety net.
- A Wing-Back or Playmaking Wing-Back on the same side that pushes high.
- A Defensive Midfielder in the centre who stays put, so the middle is not left empty when he moves wide.

## Common mistakes

- Using him with a Holding Full-Back or a Full-Back who stays back. There is nothing to cover.
- Using the same role on both sides of the midfield, leaving the centre empty.
- Picking a low Work Rate or Pace player. He will always arrive late.

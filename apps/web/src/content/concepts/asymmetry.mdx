Different sides are not a flaw: a safe full-back (IFB) on one side and an attacking wing-back (WB/AWB) on the other is one of the most stable structures. Put the attacking side on your best player’s flank.

Think of asymmetry as dividing the labour. On the safe side, an Inside Full-Back or Holding Full-Back tucks in and helps form the rest-defence, which frees the opposite Advanced Wing-Back or Wing-Back to go all the way to the byline. The two sides then do different jobs instead of mirroring each other, and the team is not stretched equally thin on both flanks.

Put the attacking side where your best creator or winger plays, and let the winger on the safe side be an Inside Winger or Playmaking Winger who comes inside rather than a second wide runner. Avoid giving both full-backs maximum attacking roles unless you have three real rest-defenders and a protective holding midfielder behind them.
